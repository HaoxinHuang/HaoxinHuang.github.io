02Research

Current project

How stiff is a bacterial flagellum?

Bacterial flagella bend and twist when a cell tumbles or flicks, but their stiffness is still not known precisely. We tether a single flagellum in a microfluidic channel and deform it with a known shear flow. I built a fast physics-based model, a Kirchhoff rod coupled to regularized-Stokeslet hydrodynamics near a wall, that predicts the flagellum's shape and its microscope image. Matching predicted and measured images gives the bending and twisting stiffness together.

  • Kirchhoff rods
  • Regularized Stokeslets
  • Microfluidics
  • Image-based inference

Previous work

2021 – 2024

A bacterial rotor in a crowd

A small chiral rotor, self-assembled at a water–air interface, is driven by bacterial flagella. In a denser bacterial suspension the rotor turns faster, up to three times, and the speed-up is nonlinear. The rotor also breaks up the vortices of the surrounding active turbulence.
